# The laptop I'd buy with my own money in 2026

I review a lot of laptops. Most of them are fine. The M5 MacBook Pro is not fine — it's exceptional in a way that's easy to take for granted until you go back to anything else.

I've been using one as my primary machine for eight weeks. This is what actually happened.

## The daily experience

The thing about a great laptop is that you stop noticing it. The M5 is the first laptop in years where I've forgotten it's a laptop — not because it's flashy, but because everything just **works**:

- Open the lid, it's on (instant, no spin-up)
- 12-hour days on battery without thinking about the charger
- No fan noise during 90% of my workflow (VS Code, browser, Slack, Figma)
- Stays cool on my lap even when I'm pushing it

When I switched back to my old Intel machine for a day last week, the difference was embarrassing. The Intel machine sounded like a hairdryer.

## Where the M5 actually wins

The M5 (vs. M3 and M4) has one specific superpower that mattered for my workflow: **sustained multi-core performance**. The M3 would thermal-throttle after a few minutes of heavy compile. The M5 holds its clock for as long as the battery lasts.

Concrete examples:

- A 200k-line TypeScript build: **42 seconds** on M5, **1:18** on my M3
- A 4K video export (10 min timeline): **6:30** on M5, **11:45** on M3
- Running our affiliate ETL in parallel with three docs open: **no perceptible lag** on M5, **occasional stutter** on M3

If your laptop spends more time plugged in than on battery, none of this matters. If you ever edit video, compile code, or render anything locally, the M5 is a real upgrade.

## Where it falls short

Three complaints worth registering:

**1. 16GB RAM on the base model.**
In 2026, for $1,999, 16GB is stingy. Bump to 24GB at minimum. The SSD is fast enough that 16GB is workable, but you're one big Chrome session away from swap.

**2. Webcam is still mediocre.**
Apple ships the best hardware and an embarrassing webcam. At this price tier, an iPhone-quality front camera should be table stakes. It's not.

**3. Ports.**
Two USB-C, one MagSafe, one HDMI, one SD card slot. Manageable, but you'll want a hub eventually. Apple's hub tax is real.

## Who should buy it

**Yes, if:**
- You create things on a laptop and time matters
- You carry your laptop to multiple places daily
- You have any kind of "big" workload (code, video, design, data)

**No, if:**
- You're doing email + browser + Slack (an M2 Air will do the same job for $700 less)
- You're on a tight budget and the M4 is discounted
- You need Windows or Linux native

## The configurator tax

The base $1,999 model is fine. But you'll be tempted:

| Upgrade | Cost | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|
| 24GB RAM | +$200 | **Yes**, do this first |
| 512GB → 1TB SSD | +$200 | Yes, if you edit video locally |
| M5 Pro chip | +$200 | Only if you regularly max out M5 |
| Nano-texture display | +$150 | Yes, for outdoor use |

I'd spec it as: **24GB / 512GB / standard display / M5 chip** at $2,199. Anything beyond is for specific workloads.

## The honest verdict

It's the best laptop you can buy in 2026, full stop. Apple's pricing is high but not unreasonable for what you get. The only real question is whether you need this much laptop, or whether an Air (M4 or M5) would serve you just as well.

If you're upgrading from an Intel Mac or any pre-2022 laptop, you'll feel the difference within an hour. If you're on an M3 or M4, only upgrade if you hit real performance walls — the M5 is iterative for you.
